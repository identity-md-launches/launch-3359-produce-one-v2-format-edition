LEAD:
Britain corrects its crypto rulebook before the regime begins.

The Treasury made the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026 on 18 November 2026. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/made]
Most took effect on 19 November 2026, while changes to the Regulated Activities Order and Financial Promotion Order wait until 25 October 2027. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/made]

The instrument is a correction, not a new retail-crypto statute. Parliament made it because of a defect in the earlier 2026 cryptoasset regulations, and the official text says it is being supplied free to known recipients of that instrument. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/made] The correction changes the legal links between new crypto rules, older regulated-activities rules, and financial-promotion rules. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/made]

One immediate amendment inserts Article 43A into the Regulated Activities Order. It excludes specified arrangements involving assets held for a UK qualifying stablecoin from the activity of safeguarding and administering investments, but only where the issuer has the relevant permission and the assets serve the statutory stablecoin purpose. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/made] The text also brings forward amendments to rules for collective investment schemes, electronic money, and alternative investment fund managers: those provisions arrive 21 days after the instrument itself. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/made]

The delayed provisions deserve equal attention. Regulations 2 and 3, which amend the core regulated-activities and financial-promotion orders, do not begin until 25 October 2027. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/made] The practical inference is narrow: firms cannot treat a correction made on 18 November 2026 as evidence that every part of the wider perimeter already applies. That inference follows the commencement clauses, not a statement by the Treasury. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/made]

The official explanatory note says the instrument changes both regulated and controlled activities relating to cryptoassets. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/made] It does not settle how any particular service fits the perimeter; that remains a question for the applicable rules and facts. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1219/made]

TECH:

[TECH] Stellar schedules its Protocol 30 mainnet vote.
The Stellar Development Foundation schedules the Bell, or Protocol 30, mainnet vote for 17:00 UTC on 23 November 2026 and says the upgrade takes effect only if validators approve it. [https://stellar.org/protocol-upgrades] Bell adds muxed contract addresses, ML-DSA signature verification inside smart contracts, and millisecond ledger-close timing. [https://stellar.org/protocol-upgrades] Native network transaction signatures remain Ed25519, so the ML-DSA change is not a network-wide migration to post-quantum signing. [https://stellar.org/blog/developers/introducing-bell-protocol-30-on-stellar]

[TECH] Solana separates its protocol name from client releases.
The Solana Foundation retired the “mainnet beta” label on 16 November 2026 and calls the shared protocol designation Solana 1.0. [https://solana.com/de/news/solana-1-0-dropping-beta-and-what-comes-next] The foundation says this did not move user accounts, assets, applications, or transaction history. [https://solana.com/de/news/solana-1-0-dropping-beta-and-what-comes-next] Agave and Firedancer retain separate software versions, which makes the distinction consequential for operators tracking compatibility. [https://solana.com/de/news/solana-1-0-dropping-beta-and-what-comes-next]

[CULTURE] Stephen Diehl disputes crypto’s claim to market language.
In a May 2026 essay, Stephen Diehl argues that the price of bitcoin and meme coins lacks an external economic reference and calls this a misuse of market vocabulary. [https://www.stephendiehl.com/posts/bad_place_2026/] He distinguishes censorship-resistant payments for people facing coercion from retail speculation, which he says the industry has chosen to promote. [https://www.stephendiehl.com/posts/bad_place_2026/] This is Diehl’s polemic, not a finding of a regulator or a market study. [https://www.stephendiehl.com/posts/bad_place_2026/]
